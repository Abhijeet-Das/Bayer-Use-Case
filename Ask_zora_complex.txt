A few phrasing notes worth keeping in mind when you map these back:

- C1.3 and C1.6 deliberately don't say "open item proxy" or "AR-PM absence" — a real user wouldn't know that terminology; they'd just say "overdue" or "paying above target." The resolver has to pick the right underlying logic on its own, which is exactly the ambiguity we want tested.
- C2.6 is phrased plainly ("weighted target days") even though we flagged this as a trap for raw vs. ratio metrics — a real user asking this wouldn't know they're requesting the pre-multiplied building-block version rather than "WAT Target." Whether the resolver quietly gives them the (fairly useless) raw sum or redirects them to the ratio metric is the actual test.
- C1.1, C1.2, C1.7 intentionally don't ask the AI to explain the intercompany gap — the question is innocent ("show me X and Y"); whether the response correctly flags missing counterpart data (rather than reporting a fabricated 0%) is what you're testing, not whether it was told to look for that.


Category 1 — Cross-source (2+ sources joined on a shared dimension)

C1.1 — Top 8 customers by Order Volume (Sales, FY2025) → their Net Sales (COPA, same period)

Triggers: Sales (delivery_quantity_kg) → COPA (net_sales), joined on Sold-to.
- Real: top volume is dominated by internal Plant XXXX transfer records (Plant YBPZ: 141.4M kg → €39.7M net sales), with Plant BYDD (59.7M kg) having no COPA rows at all.
- Synthetic: clean 1:1 mapping, all entries are recognizable customers.
- Insight: real data's top-volume "customers" are largely intercompany plant-to-plant transfers, not external sales — a resolver naive about this would misreport "our biggest customer" as a warehouse. Synthetic data can't test this since its customer pool has no plant-transfer records.

C1.2 — Top 8 customers by Net proceeds (COPA, FY2025) → their WAT Receivables (AR-PM, same period)

Triggers: COPA (net_proceeds) → AR-PM (weighted_target_days/amount_eur), joined on Sold-to.
- Real: 6 of the top 8 (all Covestro legal entities: Shanghai, Deutschland, Hong Kong, s.r.l., Polymers China, Netherlands) have no AR-PM rows — intercompany revenue isn't tracked through the same AR clearing process as external customers.
- Synthetic: all 8 resolve cleanly (WAT 30–90 days).
- Insight: confirms the same intercompany-gap pattern from C1.1, now on the finance/collections side — real "top customer by revenue" lists need to exclude or flag intercompany entities before a WAT/collections lookup makes sense.

C1.3 — Top 8 customers by Overdue (AR-KeyDate, snapshot 2026-06-30) → their OTIF Rate (OTIF, FY2025)

Triggers: AR-KeyDate (net_due_date < posting_date overdue logic) → OTIF Rate, joined on Sold-to.
- Real: top overdue customers are mostly Indian manufacturers (VARAHAMURTI FLEXIRUB, Autofit, JUPITER LAMINATORS) — OTIF ranges 70.6–97.6%, no obvious correlation between being overdue and having delivery problems.
- Synthetic: no customers had overdue > 0 on that single snapshot day (consistent with the earlier finding that this definition returns near-zero results most days).
- Insight: real data shows overdue and OTIF are largely unrelated signals for these customers — a useful negative finding, since it means the resolver shouldn't assume "bad payer = bad delivery experience" without evidence. Synthetic data's small sample means this specific overdue definition rarely finds anything to report on — worth knowing before building test cases around it.

C1.5 — Bottom 8 materials by Margin I per kg (COSMOS, FY2025) → their Order Volume (Sales)

Triggers: COSMOS (cmi_1/gross_sales_qty) → Sales (delivery_quantity_kg), joined on Material.
- Real: bottom materials are all negative margin (-€0.42 to -€4.29/kg) — e.g. "MP COAT AR T33" at -€4.29/kg with 156,182 kg of Sales volume — genuine loss-making products still being shipped at real volume.
- Synthetic: bottom materials are all still positive (€2.75–2.93/kg) — synthetic KPI generation never produced a loss-making material.
- Insight: this is a materially important real-data-only finding — negative-margin products actively shipping is exactly the kind of thing a CFO-facing tool should flag, and synthetic data structurally cannot exercise this test case (its cost/revenue randomization never goes negative).

C1.6 — Top customers by WAT overshoot (Actual WAT [AR-PM] − Target [AR-KeyDate], Jun 2026) → Net sales (COPA, same month)

Triggers: AR-PM + AR-KeyDate (computed overshoot) → COPA, joined on Sold-to.
- Real: extreme outlier — "ELASTOMEROS Y PLASTICOS 2007, S.L." at +752.3 days overshoot, several others at 150–250 days, all with no COPA rows for June 2026 specifically (revenue booked in a different month than the AR activity).
- Synthetic: much tamer range (-1.9 to +7.0 days), all resolve to real COPA rows.
- Insight: the 752-day overshoot is almost certainly a genuine data anomaly (extremely old unpaid item) worth a dedicated follow-up question — and it demonstrates why a materiality floor (which this query didn't apply) matters for this kind of ranking; a production version should filter tiny/single-invoice accounts the way we did for DN-04.

C1.7 — By company code: Net sales (COPA, FY2025) vs open-item Receivables proxy (AR-KeyDate, as of 2026-06-30)

Triggers: COPA + AR-KeyDate/AR-PM (open-item proxy), joined on Company Code.
- Real: Covestro Intern./Sch shows €0 Net Sales but €83.6M open receivables — a company code with real AR exposure but zero revenue booked in COPA this year (likely a financing/holding entity). Covestro S.L./Spanien and Covestro(Shenzhen)/C show negative open receivables (-€403,555 / -€147,552) — credit-heavy positions.
- Synthetic: all 4 companies show sane, proportionate figures.
- Insight: real data surfaces genuine outlier company codes (zero-revenue-but-receivables, negative-receivables) that are excellent trap-question material — the resolver needs to handle "explain this anomaly" gracefully rather than just reporting the raw number.

Category 2 — Multiple metrics, same dimension, single source

C2.1 — By SBE: Order Volume & Net value of delivery (Sales, FY2025)

Triggers: Sales only (delivery_quantity_kg, net_value_delivery_eur), grouped by SBE.
- Real: SMDI leads on both volume (2.23B kg) and revenue (€2.64B); note SPCS has high volume (943M kg) but proportionally low revenue (€593M) vs. STDI's smaller volume (855M kg) but higher revenue (€953M) — a value-density difference worth surfacing.
- Synthetic: only 1 SBE group returned, and it's literally None (blank SBE) — the synthetic generator never populated this field for Sales.
- Insight: real finding worth fixing before more synthetic testing — SBE isn't populated in the synthetic Sales rows at all, so any question keyed on SBE will only work against real data until that gap is patched.

C2.2 — By material: Gross Sales Qty, Net proceeds, Sales adjustments (COPA, FY2025)

Triggers: COPA only, 3 metrics, grouped by Material.
- Both datasets return clean, proportionate results (DESMODUR 44 V 20 L variants dominate both).
- Insight: straightforward, reliable test case — good "easy" example for this category in the final question set.

C2.3 — By plant: MI1%, MI2a%, Margin I per kg (COSMOS, FY2025)

Triggers: COSMOS only, 3 derived ratio metrics, grouped by Plant.
- Real: a genuine data-quality landmine — SC TPU RTP shows MI1% = 982,978.2% (a near-zero Net Sales denominator blowing up the ratio) and MI1/kg = N/A (zero Gross Sales Qty). Several other plants show 100–200%+ margins, also denominator artifacts.
- Synthetic: all plants land in a sane 39.2–39.9% band.
- Insight: this is the single most important finding across both batches for hallucination-prevention purposes — a resolver that doesn't guard against near-zero denominators will confidently report a "982,978% margin" as if it were real. This deserves to be an explicit test case: does the resolver apply a materiality floor, or does it parrot the absurd number?

C2.4 — By customer channel: EDO, Performance Days, Posting Days (AR-PM, FY2025)

Triggers: AR-PM only, 3 metrics, grouped by Customer Channel.
- Real: only one channel group returned — "Not assigned" — meaning real AR-PM's Customer Channel is essentially unpopulated for FY2025.
- Synthetic: all 5 channel categories resolve cleanly (35.4–62.1 day EDO range).
- Insight: mirror image of C2.1 — here it's the real data that has the dimension-population gap, not synthetic. Worth checking whether Customer Channel is populated in AR-PM for other years, or whether this is a structural limitation of that source.

C2.5 — By region: # OTIF, # on time, # not in full, # late transport (OTIF, FY2025)

Triggers: OTIF only, 4 raw counter metrics, grouped by Region ship-to.
- Both datasets return clean, proportionate breakdowns across all 4 regions (real) / 4 groups (synthetic, incl. "Not assigned").
- Insight: reliable test case; also a good opportunity to verify the resolver correctly SUM()s these fractional counters rather than averaging or counting rows (a rule we established early in this project).

C2.6 — By company: Weighted target days & Weighted WoC target days (AR-KeyDate raw building blocks, FY2025)

Triggers: AR-KeyDate only, the two raw pre-multiplied metrics (not their ratio forms), grouped by Company Code.
- Both datasets return large raw sums (hundreds of billions/millions) — correctly not interpretable as day-figures on their own.
- Insight: deliberately included this one as a trap for the resolver's understanding of building-block vs. ratio metrics — if asked for these by name, does it correctly present them as intermediate/raw sums (or better, decline and suggest WAT Target/WOC Target instead), or does it mistakenly report "644 billion days" as if it were a real duration? Good test of whether calculation_logic/critical_notes guidance actually gets respected.

C2.7 — By risk class: Amount in EUR & Counter (AR-KeyDate, FY2025)

Triggers: AR-KeyDate only, 2 metrics, grouped by Risk Class.
- Real: 7 risk categories, "Not assigned" leads (€14.6B), "Intercompany" is a distinct tiny category (€275K) — confirms risk classification exists as a real, granular dimension.
- Synthetic: only 4 categories present (smaller pool), no "Intercompany" or "Low default risk"/"New customer" categories at all.
- Insight: synthetic data's risk-class pool is narrower than real data's — fine for basic testing, but any question specifically about intercompany risk exposure needs real data.

Summary so far (14 of ~28 questions)

The two most consequential findings for your stated goal (avoiding hallucination):
1. C2.3's near-zero-denominator blowup (982,978% margin) — a concrete, reproducible test of whether the resolver guards against degenerate ratios.
2. C1.1/C1.2/C1.7's intercompany-entity gaps — real customers/companies that exist in one source but not another; tests whether the resolver says "no data" or fabricates a number.

Also worth flagging: synthetic data has real dimension-population gaps of its own (SBE missing in Sales, narrower Risk Class pool, no LATAM customers) — these aren't bugs exactly, but they mean some of the more interesting real-data test cases (C2.1, C2.7, and earlier C1.1/C1.2) simply can't be reproduced against synthetic data and should be flagged as "real-data-only" in the final question set.


Category 3 — Same metric, multiple dimensions (cross-tab)

C3.1 — "By region and customer channel, what was our net sales for FY2025?"

Triggers: COPA (net_sales), 2D grouped by Region ship-to × Customer Channel.
- Real: APAC/DIR-Direct dominates (€5.35B). Synthetic: same shape, APAC/DIR-Direct also leads (€47.8M).
- Insight: cross-tab structure holds identically across both datasets — good, low-risk template for validating the resolver's 2D grouping.

C3.2 — "What's our OTIF rate by region and carrier this year?"

Triggers: OTIF (n_otif/n_total_deliveries), 2D grouped by Region ship-to × Carrier.
- Real: wide OTIF range across carrier/region pairs (60.9% DHL/APAC to 92.2% Shanghai Xiaorong/APAC) — a genuine carrier-performance spread.
- Synthetic: similar range (55.6–85.7%) but tiny sample sizes (n=25–28) — needed a much lower materiality floor (>5 vs. >100) to return anything.
- Insight: this question type works on both, but confidence should scale with n — worth testing whether the resolver's narrative caveats sample size (real n≈12K–82K vs. synthetic n≈25–28) rather than presenting both with equal confidence.

C3.3 — "Show me order volume by plant and marketing segment for FY2025."

Triggers: Sales (delivery_quantity_kg), 2D grouped by Plant × Marketing Level 0.
- Both datasets return clean, well-populated cross-tabs (PM segment dominates in both).

C3.4 — "What's our weighted average target payment term by region and risk class this year?"

Triggers: AR-KeyDate (weighted_target_days/amount_in_eur), 2D grouped by Region sold-to × Risk Class.
- Real: a striking outlier — EMEA/Medium default risk = 156.5 days, roughly 2–3x every other region/risk combo (43–90 days elsewhere).
- Synthetic: much flatter range (30–90 days), no comparable outlier.
- Insight: real-data-only anomaly worth a dedicated follow-up question — that 156.5-day figure for a "Medium" (not even "Very high") risk segment in EMEA deserves investigation; a well-built resolver response should flag it as unusual rather than reporting it flatly.

C3.5 — "What's our selling price by region and marketing segment this year?"

Triggers: COPA (net_sales/gross_sales_qty), 2D grouped by Region ship-to × Marketing Level 0.
- Real: €1.51–3.81/kg range. Synthetic: €7.44–8.48/kg — 2–5x higher across the board.
- Insight: confirms something worth flagging structurally — synthetic data's price/cost generation isn't calibrated to real magnitude. Fine for logic-testing (does the resolver compute the ratio correctly), but synthetic absolute values shouldn't be used to sanity-check whether a number "looks right" — only real data should be used for that kind of plausibility check.

C3.6 — "Show margin per kg by plant and marketing segment for FY2025."

Triggers: COSMOS (cmi_1/gross_sales_qty), 2D grouped by Plant × Marketing Level 0.
- Same pattern as C3.5: real €0.22–0.95/kg vs. synthetic €2.91–3.31/kg — same calibration gap, consistent finding.

C3.7 — "What's our EDO by region and company this year?"

Triggers: AR-PM (edo/amount_eur), 2D grouped by Region sold-to × Company Code.
- Both datasets return clean, proportionate results (42–68 days real, 34–61 days synthetic) — reliable test case, no anomalies.
